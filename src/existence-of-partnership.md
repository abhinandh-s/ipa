### Sec. 6 - Determination of Existence of Partnership

To know 
1. Whether a group of persons <i class="fa-solid fa-people-group"></i> is or is not a firm; OR
2. Whether a person <i class="fa-solid fa-person"></i> is or is not a partner in a firm.

Look at the real intentions of the parties, based on all facts, not just what's on the paper or what a single factor suggests.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Sharing of profits/gross returns alone does not make them partners. The ultimate test is Mutual Agency (acts of one partner bind the others).


If a person receives
1. A share of the profit of a business; OR
2. A payment -
    - contingent upon the earning of profit; OR
    - varying with the profit earned by a business
doesn't make him a partner with the persons carrying on a business.

And in particular, a share of profit/payment received by -

| received by                       | Form of                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| lender                            | any form                                                       |
| servant/agent                     | remuneration                                                   |
| widow/child of a deceased partner | annuity                                                        |
| previous owner/part owner         | payment as consideration for the sale of the goodwill or share |
…does not make the receiver a partner with the persons carrying on a business.



> [!Court Rulings]
> - A document is not necessary to support a claim, Partnership can be oral
> - Whether a relationship of partners exists or not depends on what was intended by parties
> - Partnership Firm is not a person, but merely a collective name for the individuals who are members.
> - Since Partnership isn't a person, it cannot be a partner in another Partnership Firm
> - A partnership firm is not a separate legal entity.
